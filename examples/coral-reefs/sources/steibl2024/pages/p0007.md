The heterogenous atoll landscape. (a) Distinct environmental and disturbance–exposure gradients run across an atoll island from
lagoon- to ocean-side resulting in a zonation of its terrestrial biota. In addition, the relative position of an atoll island within the atoll
landscape creates a mosaic of equilibrium assembly points due to different exposures to atoll-scale environmental gradients. (b) Atoll
island assembly is a dynamic and not unidirectional process, with islands being repeatedly reset into earlier stages of species assembly
due to recurring natural disturbances. Islands within one atoll that are most exposed to stressors, e.g., due to the predominant wave and
wind directions, remain trapped in a certain stage of assembly ( 1⃝– 4⃝), as frequent overwash does not enable the establishment of less
halophilic species.
