6.5. Beyond the Reef: Upwelling and Pelagic Export
Outside the atoll, oceanic upwelling drives cold, nutrient-rich waters into the euphotic layer.
When these deep-sea upwelled waters on the outer margins of atolls mix with the warm, nutrient-
enriched lagoonal waters that are flushed out of the lagoon, a highly productive nearshore
environment can be created that can enhance phytoplankton biomass by up to 130% around atolls
over open-ocean conditions (Vollbrecht et al. 2021). This phenomenon, referred to as the atoll
mass effect (or in more general terms, the island mass effect) is a near-universal phenomenon oc-
curring around most atolls (Messié et al. 2022). The plankton blooms driven by the atoll mass
effect can sometimes be exported hundreds of kilometers into the open ocean, e.g., by mon-
soonal forcing, or remain trapped in stationary eddies that form around atolls (Kämpf et al. 2023).
Seabirds, sharks, and sea turtles, as well as the recruitment of terrestrial species that have a plank-
tonic larval stage, ultimately transport marine nutrients back into the atoll environment (Graham
et al. 2018, Williams et al. 2018).
6.6. Endo-Upwelling
In addition to the classical upwelling phenomenon around atolls, oceanic water from depths of
500–1,000 m is also upwelled internally through the porous atoll limestone structure, a phe-
nomenon termed endo-upwelling. The endo-upwelled water exits the limestone as interstitial
water into the lagoon or the groundwater lens of islands. Endo-upwelled waters can be 7–20 times
more enriched in inorganic phosphates and nitrates than oceanic surface waters (Rougerie et al.
1991). Cyanobacteria, which can form stromatolite mats in the brackish and freshwater ponds
on atoll islands, are putative recipients of this endo-upwelling ( Jehl & Barsczus 1996). Whether
endo-upwelling is a relevant subsidy of inorganic elements remains unclear. Flow rates through
the limestone structure suggest only minor subsidies relative to other processes (Tribble et al.
1994).
