quate depositional settings for the formation of atoll rim islands (Ortiz & Ashton 2019). Receding
sea levels, such as those that occurred after the mid-Holocene highstand in some oceanic regions,
supported the formation of islands along the rim in certain settings but were not a prerequisite
(Kench et al. 2023).
Most atolls have passed through at least five of these geo-ecological cycles since the late Quater-
nary, in accordance with the Mid-Brunhes Glaciations (Ohde et al. 2002). The periods of complete
atoll submergence were only short-lived, as coral reefs rapidly caught up with rising sea levels.
Atolls are estimated to have existed with terrestrial landforms (either islands along the rim as ob-
served today, or as Makatea-type islands) for 85–92% of the time since the late Quaternary (Gray
et al. 1992).
2.4. Emergence of Contemporary Atoll Islands
The rim island configuration observed today is a transient island morphology tied to marine
transgression events. Most atoll islands observed today have formed in the last 1,000–7,000 years,
during or after the mid-Holocene sea-level highstand (Kench et al. 2023). Because sea levels and
oceanographic conditions in the Holocene changed regionally at different rates, atoll islands across
the Indo-Pacific did not emerge concomitantly but over several thousand years (Grossman et al.
1998). The asynchronous reemergence and formation of atoll islands throughout the Indo-Pacific
(as early as 7,000 years BP or as late as 800 years BP) may also be a factor in the staged human
colonization into Oceania, and particularly Polynesia (Dickinson 2003).
Even though most atoll islands formed within the last millennia, a few atolls, such as Aldabra,
Kiritimati, and Niau, have persisted with landforms since ca. 120,000 years BP (i.e., the penul-
timate marine transgression). For these atolls, regional tectonic uplift maintained emerged
landforms during the Holocene marine transgression (Montaggioni et al. 2023). Other limestone
