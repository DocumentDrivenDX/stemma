Below 62 feet from the Lagoon floor the character of the materials from the boring
changes greatly, and in the next sample, from the depth of 75 feet, we find, instead
of a mass mainly of free, uncemented Halimeda-joints and foraminifera, fragments
of whitish or whitish-grey, rough, and porous limestone, consisting of these
organisms cemented by calcite into fairly hard rock. At this level the //alimeda is
no longer so predominant as in the higher part of the boring and approximately only
about one-third of the rock consists of it, while the foraminifera, greatly increased in
numbers, but mostly of the same genera and species, constitute the other two-thirds.
The same kind of porous rubbly limestone, now reduced in the sample to angular
fragments and fine powder, continues to the bottom of the boring at 144 feet, but im
the lower 50 feet the proportion of corals greatly increases and the rock is mainly
composed of them and foraminifera; the Halimeda beg here reduced to a small
percentage.
The corals belong to Heliopora caerulea, Lobophytum, Pocillopora, Servatopora,
Celoria (rare), Cyphastrea, other Astrzans not determinable, Munguia, Madrepore,
including MW. contecta, Montipora, Porites, Astrwopora, and Goniopora. Perforate
corals are the most numerous. As a rule, the coral walls are preserved, and even
their minute structures are shown in microscopic sections. The interstices are usually
empty, but not infrequently they are filled up solid with crystalline material, and in
this case the walls are sometimes decomposed intoa soft white powder and partially
removed,
The foraminifera, in the lower 50 feet of the boring, chiefly belong to Polytrema,
Amphistegina, and ITcterosteyina ; other forms, recognised in microscopic sections of
rock fragments are Miliolina, Textularia, Globigerina, Planorbuiina, Discorbina,
Calearina, and Gypsinu. They are now embedded in fine consolidated sediment.
Other organisms include: Clioww borings in corals, aleyonarian spicules, echinid
spines and plates, Serpula, Spirorbis, spicules of Leptoclinum, Thecidea, polyzoa,
small gastropods and lamellibranchs, and lastly, branching and _ encrusting
LInthothammon. .
