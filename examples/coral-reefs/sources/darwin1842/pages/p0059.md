Beginning with the outside of the reef; many scattered
soundings off Gambier, Oualan, and some other encircled
islands, show that close to the breakers there exists a narrow
shelving margin, beyond which the ocean becomes sud-
denly unfathomable : but off the west coast of New Cale-
donia, Capt Kent* found no bottom with 150 fathoms,
at two ships’ length from the reef; so that the slope here
must be nearly as precipitous as off the Maldiva atolls.
I can give little information regarding the kinds of corals
which live on the outer margin. When I visited the reef at
Tahiti, although it was low water, the surf was too violent
for me to see the living masses ; but, according to what I
heard from some intelligent native chiefs, they resemble
in their rounded and branchless forms, those on the margin
of Keeling atoll. The extreme verge of the reef, which was
visible between the breaking waves at low water, consisted
of a rounded, convex, artificial-like breakwater, entirely
coated with Nulliporse, and absolutely similar to that which
I have described at Keeling atoll. From what I heard
when at Tahiti, and from the writings of the Bevs. W. Ellis
and J. Williams, I conclude that this peculiar structure
is common to most of the encircled islands of the Society
Archipelago. The reef within this mound or breakwater,
has an extremely irregular surface, even more so than between
the islets on the reef of Keeling atoll, with which* alone (as
there are no islets on the reef of Tahiti) it can properly
be compared. At Tahiti, the reef is very irregular in width
