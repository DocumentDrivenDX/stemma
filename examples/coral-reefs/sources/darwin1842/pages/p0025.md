I examined the rolled fragments cast on the beach during

the reef. The fragments consisted of many kinds, of
which the Porites already mentioned and a Madrepora,
apparently the M. corymhosa, were the most abundant.
As I searched in vain in the hollows on the reef and
in the lagoon, for a living specimen of this Madrepore,
I conclude that it is confined to a zone outside, and be-
neath the surface, where it must be very abundant. Frag-
ments of the Millepora alcicornis and of an Astrsea Avere
also numerous ; the former is found, but not in proportionate
numbers, in the hollows on the reef ; but the Astrsea I did
not see living. Hence we may infer, that these are the
kinds of coral which form the rugged sloping surface, (re-
presented in the wood cut by an uneven line) round and be-
