How it works

You ask in plain words. Claude does the clerical work, scripts do the comparing, and you make the decisions.

The research cycle

One round, step by step

  1. Start a project

    You might say “Start a Stemma project in this folder.”

    Claude creates a few project files and overwrites nothing.

    One of them, rules.md, holds your standing rules, such as how to spell a place name. Claude is instructed to read it at the start of each session.

  2. Gather your sources

    You might say “Add a source record for this book, taking the details from its title page.”

    Claude writes a record of the work and tells you where each detail came from. It is instructed never to invent one.

  3. Keep your copies

    You might say “Keep this PDF as my copy of that source. I downloaded it from the library today.”

    The file goes into your private store, with where and when you got it, and a digital fingerprint so that any later change shows.

    You can also note that material is unpublished, restricted by a community, or under a particular license. These notes inform you; they do not stop anything being sent to the AI model.

  4. Read the text and number the pages

    You might say “Read the text of that copy and work out its page numbers.”

    Stemma reads the text a PDF already contains, or takes a plain text file. It cannot read words from a picture of a page: a scan with no text stays unchecked until you supply its text.

    It proposes page numbers only where the numbers printed on the pages support them. Someone, you or Claude, reviews the proposal before it is used.

  5. Write, and record what you cite

    You might say “Record the citations in this chapter.”

    Each citation gets its own record: the sentence, the source, the page and any quotation, exactly as you wrote it.

    You can ask to see a few pages while you write. They are marked as text quoted from the source, which may contain errors.

  6. Check

    You might say “Check the project and explain what is open.”

    The whole project is checked. Claude explains what is wrong, shows the evidence, says why it matters and suggests what to do.

    A check that cannot be done is reported as could not check. It never counts as a pass.

  7. Decide, and go round again

    You might say “I have a better scan of this book. Replace the old one.”

    You choose what to fix. When a copy is replaced, you see what relied on the old one. This version cannot note that you have looked again, so those items stay listed.

    Ask Is anything missing from my source store? to find files that have gone missing or changed.

What going round looks like

In the demo, one project is checked five times. The numbers rise and fall as things are fixed, added and lost.

Where a finding sends you

When a check reportsGo back to
A cited source has no copy, or its copy has no textKeep your copies, or read the text
A quotation differs from your copyLook at the page, then correct your record or your sentence
A cited page is not in the workCheck the citation, then the page numbering
A citation does not point to one sourceCorrect the citation or the source record
Something relied on a copy you replacedCompare it with the new copy

Where your material lives

Words used here

Record
A small plain file about one thing: a source, a copy, its text or a citation.
Stored copy
Your file of the work, such as a PDF, kept in your private store.
Page map
A list that says which page of the file carries which printed page number.
Finding
One thing a check reports, with its evidence and a suggestion.
Could not check
Something needed was missing, so there is no answer yet.
Checked clean
The checks found nothing open. It does not mean the book is correct. A project that cites sources does not get this label, because some kinds of check do not run.
Unreviewed, not admitted
A report counts cited items that nobody has reviewed, and sources you have not accepted into your project. The count is information, not a finding.
