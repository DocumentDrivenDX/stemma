Research and write with Claude. The Stemma plugin keeps your sources in your project, checks your citations and quotations against them, and explains what it finds in plain language.

Why rigor takes more

An AI assistant can produce a reference that does not exist, give a real title the wrong author, cite a page the work does not have, or invent a quotation.

Checking all of that by hand can cost the time the assistant saved. With Stemma, the comparing that a computer can do reliably is done for you, against your own sources. The report also tells you what it could not check, so you know where to look yourself.

Research in rounds

Research is rarely finished in one pass. You find another source, get a better scan, or rewrite a sentence. Stemma fits that loop.

How a round works, step by step

Every citation has a family tree

Scholars draw a stemma codicum to show which copy of a manuscript comes from which. Stemma does something similar for your work: each citation leads back to the words and page it came from, to the text of the source in your project, and to the original that text was made from.

What gets checked

Your sources
Is each work you cite in your project, with its text complete and unchanged? If you replace one, you see what relied on the old one.
Your quotations
Are the words in your source's text, on the page you cite? If something close is there instead, you see both side by side.
Your page numbers
Is each page you cite actually in the work, and do its printed page numbers run on sensibly?
Your source details
Do authors, titles and dates agree with the title page and with saved library catalogue records? Where they differ, you see both.
Your references
Does each citation point to one source? Is each work you list actually cited? Is a figure you attribute to a source in its text? Are the project files in order? You are also warned about map coordinates written into them.

What stays yours

Judgment stays with you. A pass only means that your record agrees with the text made from your source, and that text can be wrong, especially when a computer read it from a scan. No check tells you that a source supports your argument.

Stemma does not compare text with pictures of pages, and it cannot read a scan itself. It does not read your manuscript files; it checks the citations that have been recorded. Nothing ever stops you from writing.
