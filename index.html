A Claude plugin for research you can trace

Create academically rigorous research reports and books

Research technical subjects with Claude: documentation for the version you name, release notes, papers and engineering write-ups. The Stemma plugin keeps their text in your project, checks your citations and quotations against it, and explains what it finds in plain language.

From an original source to a footnote

  1. Original source

    The paper, page or book as you got it, such as a PDF or a saved web page. It is not added to your project.

  2. Upstream record

    Where the original came from, and its digital fingerprint, which no other file shares.

  3. Source files

    Its text, kept in your project: one file for each page, or for each section where there are no pages.

  4. Passage

    Words you chose by their place. A script copies them, with their page, chapter or paragraph.

  5. Citation

    A record that ties your sentence to the source, with the place and any quotation.

  6. Claim

    What your report asserts, where you record it, with how sure you are and why. Notes on passages bear on it.

  7. Sentence and footnote

    The sentence in your report, with a footnote that gives the quotation and then its citation.

Why rigor takes more

An AI assistant can produce a reference that does not exist, give a real title the wrong author, cite a page the work does not have, or invent a quotation.

Checking all of that by hand can cost the time the assistant saved. With Stemma, the comparing that a computer can do reliably is done for you, against your own sources. The report also tells you what it could not check, so you know where to look yourself.

Made for researching technical subjects

  • Documentation, for the version you name

    Capture the pages for the version you are writing about, and note the version on the record. Your project keeps their text as you captured it, even if the page online changes.

  • Release notes

    Quote what changed from the notes themselves, with the release they belong to.

  • Papers

    A PDF is read page by page, and cited by its printed page numbers where those on the pages support them.

  • Engineering write-ups

    A saved article usually has no pages, so a passage in it is cited by paragraph number.

Every citation has a family tree

Scholars draw a stemma codicum to show which copy of a manuscript comes from which. Stemma does something similar for your work: each citation leads back to the words and page it came from, to the text of the source in your project, and to the original that text was made from.

What gets checked

Your sources
Is each work you cite in your project, with its text complete and unchanged? If you replace one, you see what relied on the old one.
Your quotations
Are the words in your source's text, on the page you cite? If something close is there instead, you see both side by side.
Your page numbers
Is each page you cite actually in the work, and do its printed page numbers run on sensibly?
Your source details
Do authors, titles and dates agree with the title page and with saved library catalogue records? Where they differ, you see both.
Your references
Does each citation point to one source? Is each work you list actually cited? Is a figure you attribute to a source in its text? Are the project files in order? You are also warned about map coordinates written into them.

What stays yours

Judgment stays with you. A pass only means that your record agrees with the text made from your source, and that text can be wrong, especially when a computer read it from a scan. No check tells you that a source supports your argument.

Stemma does not compare text with pictures of pages, and it cannot read a scan itself. It checks the citations in manuscript files written in Stemma's marked-up Markdown, or else the citations that have been recorded; it does not check a Word file. Nothing ever stops you from writing.
