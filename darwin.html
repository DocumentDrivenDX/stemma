Worked example: Darwin and coral reefs

One real source and four citations of it, each with a short quotation, kept as record files in the repository.

The copy of the book and its text are private and are not published. Only records and digests are. A model compared the facts and quotations below with images of the stored pages; no person has confirmed them.

The source

The Structure and Distribution of Coral Reefs. Being the First Part of the Geology of the Voyage of the Beagle, under the Command of Capt. Fitzroy, R.N. during the Years 1832 to 1836. Charles Darwin. London: Smith, Elder and Co., 1842.

The title and year were read from the image of the title page. The text layer of the same page, from unverified character recognition, gives one of the voyage's years wrongly and does not hold the year of publication.

What cites it

Each sentence is this example's own wording around a quotation. Each quotation was compared, punctuation included, with the image of the cited page.

PageQuotationCiting sentence
88The theory most generally received on the formation of atolls, is that they are based on submarine cratersDarwin opens his fifth chapter with the received view: “The theory most generally received on the formation of atolls, is that they are based on submarine craters”.
89this view inapplicable to the greater number of atollsHe calls Chamisso’s account earlier and better than the crater theory, yet finds that a difficulty of the same kind makes “this view inapplicable to the greater number of atolls”.
2I have invariably used in this volume the termOf the word atoll he writes, “I have invariably used in this volume the term”, and treats it as a synonym for lagoon-island.
89I am far from denying that a reef like a perfectly characterized atoll might not be formedHe does not rule the crater rim out altogether: “I am far from denying that a reef like a perfectly characterized atoll might not be formed”.

Page numbers

source pages proposed two runs of printed numbers in the scan. The second turned out, on its page images, to be a publisher's list of other books bound at the back, numbered on its own. It was left out of the map: a number printed on a page need not number the work. That is why a proposed page map is reviewed.

With the private copy

On the maintainer's machine on 2026-11-26, all four citations passed source.held, quote.present, locator.extent and source.changed. One quotation matched only after joining a word split across a line end, which the report points out. That run depends on the private store and is not reproduced here. A match is agreement with recognized text: a correct quotation of another sentence on page 2 was reported as a near match, because the recognized text has a stray space after a quotation mark. Even with nothing open, the project is not called checked clean: its report lists checks of cited sources that were not run.

What a check says without the private copy

Where the stored copy is not present, Stemma cannot compare the citations or quotations with anything, and says so. The page numbers can still be compared with the page map, which is in the records.
