Worked example: Darwin and coral reefs

A real book from 1842, followed from its title page to four checked quotations.

An AI model built this example and compared its facts and quotations with pictures of the pages. No person has confirmed them, and no record is marked as reviewed. The copy of the book and its full text are not published here; the records about them are.

The whole example at a glance

1. The book

The Structure and Distribution of Coral Reefs. Being the First Part of the Geology of the Voyage of the Beagle, under the Command of Capt. Fitzroy, R.N. during the Years 1832 to 1836. Charles Darwin. London: Smith, Elder and Co., 1842.

The first step is a record of the work itself. The title and year were read from the picture of the title page, which matters: the computer-read text of that same page gives one of the voyage's years wrongly and does not hold the year of publication.

The short label darwin1842 is how citations refer to this book. The book is marked as a candidate: nobody has formally accepted it into the project.

The record file: src-darwinreefs1842.json

2. The copy

The copy is a 15.8 MB scan from the Internet Archive (Internet Archive item b29331213). Its record notes the address it came from, when it was obtained, and a fingerprint of the exact file. If the file changed by even one character, the fingerprint would no longer match.

Address: https://archive.org/download/b29331213/b29331213.pdf

The file itself stays in a private store outside the project.

The record file: cap-7sskba1cqwz7975wsc1v.json

3. Its text

The PDF already contains text, made by a computer reading the page pictures. Stemma saved that text page by page and recorded how it was made. Nobody has proofread it, and the record marks its quality as not assessed. It also notes that 15 of the 258 PDF pages have no text at all.

Ask Claude for it with a request like Read the text of that copy and work out its page numbers. The record file is long, because it notes where each page's text begins and ends.

Text record: ext-kb8hzwcvz40x3yyaxnkt

4. Its page numbers

The third page of a PDF is rarely page 3 of the book. Stemma proposed page numbers from the numbers printed on the pages, and found two runs of them.

Looking at the page pictures showed that the second run belonged to a publisher's list of other books, bound in at the back with its own numbering. It was taken out. A number printed on a page does not always number the work, which is why a proposal is reviewed before it is used.

The record file: page-map.json

5. Four citations

Each sentence below is this example's own wording around a short quotation, which is underlined. Each quotation was compared, punctuation included, with the picture of the cited page.

  1. page 88

    Darwin opens his fifth chapter with the received view: “The theory most generally received on the formation of atolls, is that they are based on submarine craters”.

    cit-darwinreefs0001

  2. page 89

    He calls Chamisso’s account earlier and better than the crater theory, yet finds that a difficulty of the same kind makes “this view inapplicable to the greater number of atolls”.

    cit-darwinreefs0002

  3. page 2

    Of the word atoll he writes, “I have invariably used in this volume the term”, and treats it as a synonym for lagoon-island.

    cit-darwinreefs0003

  4. page 89

    He does not rule the crater rim out altogether: “I am far from denying that a reef like a perfectly characterized atoll might not be formed”.

    cit-darwinreefs0004

The record file: cit-darwinreefs0001.json

6. The check

With the private copy in place, the check compared all four citations with it. For each one: the copy and text are there and unchanged, the quotation is in the text, the page is within the numbered pages, and nothing it relies on has been replaced. Nothing was left open among the checks that ran.

One quotation matched only after rejoining the word denying, split at the end of a line. The report points that out so you can look.

The project is still not called checked clean, and the first line of the report says why: three kinds of check do not run. It also notes that nobody has admitted the source.

7. Planted mistakes

To see whether the checks would notice, two mistakes were made in a throwaway copy of the project: greater was changed to smaller in the second quotation, and the first citation was moved to page 412.

Both were caught. The changed quotation is shown beside the wording in the copy (the mark ⏎ shows where a line ends). For the impossible page, the report also says where the quoted words were found: page 88.

What the tool printed for the planted mistakes

A correct quotation that was flagged

One further try, noted in the example's own notes and not shown here: a correct quotation from page 2 was reported as only a near match, because the computer-read text has a stray space after a quotation mark. A finding means look at the page, not you are wrong.

8. Without the copy

Anyone can download the example's records, but not the private copy. On such a machine the check cannot compare the citations or quotations with anything, and it says could not check instead of passing them. It can still test the page numbers, because the page map is part of the records.

Where the facts come from

Below, a picture of a page means an image drawn from the stored PDF and read by an AI model (claude-sonnet-5-5). No person has read these pages for the example.

Title, author, publisher, place and year
Read from the picture of the title page. The computer-read text of that page gets one voyage year wrong (1882 for 1832) and misses the year of publication.
The four quotations
Each compared word by word, punctuation included, with the picture of its page: PDF pages 19, 105 and 106, counting from 0.
Where the copy came from
The web address it was downloaded from, as logged. The time it was obtained is the downloaded file's own date.
The text
The text already inside the Internet Archive's PDF, made by character recognition that nobody has proofread. Only the pages named here were compared with their pictures.
The page numbers
The proposal found the expected number on 191 of 213 pages. The Internet Archive's page-number file and the numbering inside the PDF agree on all 213. Pictures of four pages were looked at.

All the example's files and notes, on GitHub

What this does not show

The checks show that four recorded quotations agree with computer-read text of one copy, and that their page numbers fall within the numbered pages. They do not show that Darwin is quoted fairly, that the sentences around the quotations are right, or that this is the best edition to cite. The authors, title and date were not checked by any script.
