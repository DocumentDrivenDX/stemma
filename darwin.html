Worked example: Darwin and coral reefs

A real book from 1842, followed from its title page to four checked quotations.

An AI model built this example and compared its facts and quotations with pictures of the pages. No person has confirmed them, and no record is marked as reviewed. The example holds the text of 14 works on coral reefs. No PDF or page picture is published with it.

The whole example at a glance

1. The works

The example gathers these works in the old argument over how coral reefs form. Its four citations are of Darwin's 1842 book.

  • Charles Lyell. Principles of Geology. 1832.
  • Proceedings of the Geological Society of London. 1838.
  • Charles Darwin. The Structure and Distribution of Coral Reefs. Being the First Part of the Geology of the Voyage of the Beagle, under the Command of Capt. Fitzroy, R.N. during the Years 1832 to 1836. 1842.
  • Proceedings of the Royal Society of Edinburgh. 1880.
  • Karl Semper. Animal Life as Affected by the Natural Conditions of Existence. 1881.
  • Francis Darwin (editor). The Life and Letters of Charles Darwin, including an Autobiographical Chapter. 1887.
  • Charles Darwin. The Structure and Distribution of Coral Reefs. 1889.
  • James D. Dana. Corals and Coral Islands. 1890.
  • Alexander Agassiz. The Coral Reefs of the Tropical Pacific. 1903.
  • Coral Reef Committee of the Royal Society. The Atoll of Funafuti. 1904.
  • Reginald A. Daly. “The Glacial-Control Theory of Coral Reefs”. Proceedings of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences. 1915.
  • Harry S. Ladd and Seymour O. Schlanger. Drilling Operations on Eniwetok Atoll. 1960.
  • Blanchon and others. “Postglacial Fringing-Reef to Barrier-Reef conversion on Tahiti links Darwin’s reef types”. Scientific Reports. 2014.
  • Steibl and others. “A Renaissance of Atoll Ecology”. Annual Review of Ecology, Evolution, and Systematics. 2024.

The short label darwin1842 is how citations refer to Darwin's book. It is marked as a candidate: nobody has formally accepted it into the project.

The record file: src-darwinreefs1842.json

2. One original

The original of Darwin's book is a 15.8 MB scan from the Internet Archive (Internet Archive item b29331213). Its record notes the address it came from, when it was obtained, and a fingerprint of the exact file. If the file changed by even one character, the fingerprint would no longer match. The PDF itself is not in the project.

Address: https://archive.org/download/b29331213/b29331213.pdf

3. Its source files

The PDF already contains text, made by a computer reading the page pictures. Stemma wrote it into the project as 258 files, one for each page, blank pages included. Nobody has proofread it, and its quality is marked as not assessed. Where the layout of a page, such as its paragraphs or footnotes, could not be told, the file says so.

The PDF was then converted a second time, and every page agreed.

4. Its page numbers

The third page of a PDF is rarely page 3 of the book. Stemma took page numbers from the numbers printed on the pages, and left 45 pages without one.

Among them is a publisher's list of other books, bound in at the back with numbering of its own. Its numbers repeat the book's, so they were not used. A number printed on a page does not always number the work.

6. The check

All four citations pass: each quotation is in the source's text, on the page cited. One matched after rejoining the word denying, split at the end of a line; the report points that out so you can look.

The project as a whole is checked clean: nothing across the works is open. No person had to approve anything. The report also says how it got there. The computer-read text of Darwin's title page leaves out the year, and a saved library record of the same copy bears it out. In another volume the printed page numbers jump from 232 to 333; every page of the original is there and the text runs on, so the report records the jump as something seen in the original. Checked clean does not mean the work is correct, only that these checks found nothing wrong.

What the tool printed

7. Planted mistakes

To see whether the checks would notice, two citations in a throwaway copy of the project were changed as if an author had typed them wrongly: greater became smaller in the second quotation, and the first citation was moved to page 412.

Both were caught. The changed quotation is shown beside the wording in the source (the mark ⏎ shows where a line ends). For the impossible page, the report also says where the quoted words are: page 88.

What the tool printed for the planted mistakes

A correct quotation that would be flagged

On page 2, the computer-read text has a stray space after a quotation mark. A quotation typed correctly from the page across that mark would be reported as only a near match. A finding means look at the page, not you are wrong.

8. Checking it again

The text of every source is in the project, so anyone who downloads the example gets the same check results without the PDFs. Someone who obtains the originals can also have them converted again and compared with the published text.

Where the facts come from

Below, a picture of a page means an image drawn from an original PDF and read by an AI model (claude-sonnet-5-5). No person has read these pages for the example.

Title, author, publisher, place and year
Read from the picture of the title page. The computer-read text of that page does not hold the year of publication; the saved Internet Archive record of the same item gives it, and the check names that record as its basis.
The four quotations
An AI model chose the passages, and a script copied them. The model then compared each with the picture of its page: PDF pages 19, 105 and 106, counting from 0.
Where the original came from
The web address it was downloaded from. The time it was obtained is the downloaded file's own date.
The text
The text already inside the Internet Archive's PDF, made by character recognition that nobody has proofread.
The page numbers
Proposed from the numbers printed on the pages. They agree with the Internet Archive's page-number file and with the numbering inside the PDF wherever those give a number.
The other works
Their titles, names and years were read from pictures of their title pages or first pages.

What this does not show

The checks show that four quotations agree with computer-read text of one copy, on the pages cited. They do not show that Darwin is quoted fairly, that the sentences around the quotations are right, or that this is the best edition to cite. The other works are held but not yet cited, and no argument has been built from them here.
